Mathematical Derivation of Wind Power

Prepared by G NAGARJUNA

A step-by-step guide for students to understand the Power Equation.

The power in wind comes from the kinetic energy of moving air molecules.

The standard formula for Kinetic Energy (E) of any moving mass (m) at a specific velocity (v) is:

E = 1/2 * m * v^2

Where:

  • m: mass of the air.
  • v: velocity (speed) of the wind.

Since wind is a continuous flow, we need to calculate how much mass passes through the turbine blades over a period of time (t).

Volume (V) = Area * Distance = A * (v * t)

Mass (m) = Density * Volume = rho * A * v * t

m = rho * A * v * t

Where:

  • rho (ρ): Air density (approx 1.225 kg/m³).
  • A: Swept area of the blades.
  • v: Wind speed.

Power is defined as Energy divided by Time. We substitute our Mass formula from Step 2 into the Kinetic Energy formula from Step 1.

Power (P) = (1/2 * m * v^2) / t

Substitute m: P = (1/2 * (rho * A * v * t) * v^2) / t

The time (t) cancels out, leaving the final equation:

P = 1/2 * rho * A * v^3

Important Note: Power is proportional to the cube of the velocity. Doubling the wind speed results in 8 times more power!

A turbine cannot capture all of the wind's energy. If it did, the air would stop completely behind the turbine, and no more air could flow through.

The Betz Limit states that the maximum theoretical efficiency a turbine can achieve is 16/27.

Efficiency = 59.3%

In practice, modern turbines operate at about 35% to 45% efficiency due to mechanical friction and air turbulence.
