Complete Derivation: The Betz Limit

Prepared by G NAGARJUNA

To derive the limit, we imagine the wind turbine as an "actuator disk" that extracts energy from a stream of air.

We define three velocities:

  • v1: The speed of wind far upstream (before the turbine).
  • v: The speed of wind exactly at the turbine rotor.
  • v2: The speed of wind far downstream (after energy is extracted).

According to Axial Momentum Theory, the velocity at the rotor is the average of the upstream and downstream speeds:

v = (v1 + v2) / 2

The mass of air (m) passing through the rotor area (A) per second is known as the mass flow rate.

Mass Flow (m) = ρ · A · v

Substituting the value of v from Step 1:

m = ρ · A · (v1 + v2) / 2

According to Newton's Second Law, the Force (F) exerted by the wind on the rotor is the change in momentum:

F = m · (v1 − v2)

The Power (P) extracted is Force multiplied by Velocity at the rotor (v):

P = F · v = [m · (v1 − v2)] · v

Substituting m and v into this equation:

P = [ρ · A · (v1 + v2) / 2] · (v1 − v2) · [(v1 + v2) / 2]

Using the algebraic identity (a+b)(a-b) = a² - b², we simplify the expression:

P = (ρ · A / 4) · (v1 + v2) · (v1² − v2²)

We want to find the ratio v2 / v1 that gives the maximum power. Let this ratio be k.

Using calculus (differentiation), we find that the maximum power is captured when the wind is slowed down to exactly 1/3 of its initial speed.

v2 = 1/3 · v1

When we plug this back into the power equation and compare it to the total available power in the wind (1/2 ρ A v1³), we get the ratio:

Max Power / Total Power = 16 / 27

This numerical value 16/27 is approximately 0.593 or 59.3%.
