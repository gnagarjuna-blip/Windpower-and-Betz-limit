Physics of Wind Power Derivation

Prepared by G NAGARJUNA

Wind power is derived from the motion of air mass. The Kinetic Energy (E) of any object with mass (m) moving at velocity (v) is:

E = 1/2 * m * v²

To find the mass (m) of air passing through the turbine area (A) in a given time (t):

  • Volume (V) = Area * Distance = A * (v * t)
  • Mass (m) = Density * Volume
m = ρ * A * v * t

Where ρ (rho) is the air density.

Power (P) is Energy per unit time (E/t). Substituting the mass into the energy equation:

P = (1/2 * (ρ * A * v * t) * v²) / t

The time (t) cancels out, giving us the Total Wind Power:

P = 1/2 * ρ * A * v³

Note: Because velocity is cubed (v³), doubling the wind speed increases power by 8 times!

A turbine cannot extract 100% of the wind's power because the air must continue moving to leave the area. Using the Actuator Disk Model:

  1. Let v_upstream be initial speed and v_downstream be exit speed.
  2. Power Extracted = Difference in Kinetic Energy.
  3. Maximum power occurs when v_downstream = 1/3 * v_upstream.

This results in the Betz Limit coefficient (Cp):

Cp = 16/27 ≈ 59.3%

No turbine can capture more than 59.3% of the kinetic energy in wind.
