Complete Derivation: Wind Power and Betz Limit

Prepared by G NAGARJUNA

Part I: Fundamental Power Equation

The power available in the wind is derived from its Kinetic Energy (E). For any mass (m) moving at a velocity (v), the energy is:

E = 1/2 · m · v2

Wind is a continuous stream of air. We define the mass (m) of air passing through a rotor area (A) in time (t) as:

Mass = Density · Volume = Density · (Area · Distance)

Since Distance = Velocity · Time:

m = ρ · A · v · t

Power (P) is Energy (E) divided by Time (t). By substituting the mass equation into the energy equation:

P = (1/2 · (ρ · A · v · t) · v2) / t

The time (t) cancels out, giving us the primary wind power formula:

P = 1/2 · ρ · A · v3

Part II: The Betz Limit Derivation

To find the maximum power a turbine can extract, we look at the wind speed before (v1) and after (v2) the turbine. The speed at the rotor (v) is the average:

v = (v1 + v2) / 2

The Force (F) on the blades is the change in momentum over time:

F = m · (v1 − v2)

Since Power (P) = Force · Velocity:

P = [ρ · A · (v1 + v2) / 2] · (v1 − v2) · [(v1 + v2) / 2]

By differentiating the power equation, we find that maximum power is extracted when the downstream wind speed is 1/3 of the upstream speed (v2 = 1/3 v1).

Plugging this back in gives the maximum theoretical Power Coefficient (Cp):

Cp = 16 / 27 ≈ 59.3%
