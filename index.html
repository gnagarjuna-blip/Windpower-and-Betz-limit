Part I: Fundamental Power Equation

The power in wind is derived from Kinetic Energy (E). For an air mass (m) moving at a velocity (v):

E = 1/2 · m · v2

We consider the mass of air passing through the rotor area (A) per unit of time (t):

Mass = Density (ρ) · Area (A) · Distance (v · t)

m = ρ · A · v · t

Power (P) is Energy per unit time. Substituting m into the Energy equation:

P = (1/2 · (ρ · A · v · t) · v2) / t

The time (t) cancels, leaving the fundamental relationship:

P = 1/2 · ρ · A · v3

Part II: The Betz Limit Derivation

Using the Actuator Disk Model, let v1 be upstream speed and v2 be downstream speed. The speed at the rotor is the average:

v = (v1 + v2) / 2

The Force (F) exerted by the wind is the change in momentum:

F = m · (v1 − v2)

Power extracted is Force times velocity. To find the maximum, we use calculus to differentiate Power with respect to the speed ratio.

Maximum power occurs when v2 = 1/3 v1.

Cp, max = 16 / 27 ≈ 59.3%

Part III: Numerical Problem

Problem: A wind turbine has a blade length of 50 meters. The wind speed is 12 m/s and the air density is 1.2 kg/m3. If the turbine operates at an efficiency (Cp) of 40%, calculate the total power output in Megawatts (MW).

Step 1: Calculate the Swept Area (A)

The area is a circle: A = π · r2

A = 3.1415 · (50)2 = 3.1415 · 2500 = 7,854 m2

Step 2: Calculate the Total Available Power in Wind

Pwind = 1/2 · ρ · A · v3

Pwind = 0.5 · 1.2 · 7,854 · (12)3

Pwind = 0.6 · 7,854 · 1,728 = 8,143,488 Watts

Step 3: Calculate Actual Power Output

Pactual = Pwind · Cp

Pactual = 8,143,488 · 0.40 = 3,257,395 Watts

Final Answer: 3.26 MW
